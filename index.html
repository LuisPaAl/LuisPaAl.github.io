01 / RESEARCH OVERVIEW

RESEARCH FOCUS

From the end of inflation
to nucleosynthesis

The period between the end of inflation and Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) is not well constrained. The Universe may have experienced prolonged reheating, an early matter-dominated era or kination. Different expansion histories and matter components affect the evolution of primordial fluctuations and the conditions for black-hole formation.

My research studies how these physical conditions affect primordial-black-hole (PBH) formation and gravitational-wave (GW) production. I use analytical models and numerical relativity to study individual collapse events, then investigate how their properties enter cosmological predictions for PBH populations and GW backgrounds.

03 / RESEARCH AREAS

Research areas

My research combines early-Universe models, numerical simulations of nonlinear gravity, and predictions for PBHs and gravitational waves.

01 / CRITICAL COLLAPSE◎

Critical gravitational collapse & discrete self-similarity

How universal are the critical exponents and other features of gravitational collapse, and which of them survive in PBH observables? I use numerical relativity to investigate critical mass scaling, discrete self-similarity (DSS) and their possible imprints on PBH mass distributions.

02 / REHEATING⌁

Reheating, scalar fields & primordial structure

If reheating lasts sufficiently long, primordial fluctuations may grow into inflaton halos and compact scalar-field cores. I investigate how these structures form and evolve, and how interactions, accretion and possible collapse into PBHs could help constrain the duration and physical conditions of reheating.

03 / PBHS AND GWS〰

PBH populations & gravitational-wave backgrounds

How can PBH abundances, mass distributions and stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds help distinguish different scenarios for the early Universe? I study their dependence on primordial fluctuations, expansion history and nonlinear collapse, including how to separate features produced at formation from those inherited from the initial conditions.

04 / PARTICLE PHYSICS◇

Metastable fields, Higgs-like potentials & spacetime

What happens when a spectator field crosses a metastable barrier? Our nonlinear simulations uncover distinct black-hole-forming branches. The connection to the physical Standard Model Higgs remains an important open question.

06 / ACADEMIC BACKGROUND

Academic background

I have worked in Mexico, the United Kingdom and Japan, collaborating on theoretical cosmology, numerical relativity and gravitational-wave physics.

My path

I trained in physics at the Universidad de Guadalajara and completed a PhD at CINVESTAV–IPN in Mexico in 2020, advised by Tonatiuh Matos. During my doctorate I spent six months at UT Austin working with Paul Shapiro and Tanja Rindler-Daller.

I then worked at UNAM with Juan Carlos Hidalgo, at Queen Mary University of London with David Mulryne and Karim Malik, and now at Rikkyo University in Tokyo with Tomohiro Harada. Each step broadened the tools and physical questions I could tackle.

I also teach and mentor students, develop numerical tools and collaborate on problems in cosmology, gravitation and high-energy theory.
