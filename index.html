01 / RESEARCH OVERVIEW

RESEARCH FOCUS

From the end of inflation
to nucleosynthesis

The period between the end of inflation and Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) is not well constrained. High-energy physics may have led to departures from the standard radiation-dominated history, with well-motivated examples including slow reheating and kination. Different expansion histories and matter components affect the evolution of primordial fluctuations and the conditions for black-hole formation.

My research studies how these physical conditions affect primordial-black-hole (PBH) formation and gravitational-wave (GW) production. I use analytical models and numerical relativity to study individual collapse events, then investigate how their properties enter cosmological predictions for PBH populations and GW backgrounds.

05 / RESEARCH PROGRAMME

Research programme

A longer-term agenda linking fundamental early-Universe physics to nonlinear gravitational dynamics and potentially observable signatures.

CENTRAL QUESTION

What can primordial black holes and gravitational waves tell us about the physical conditions of the Universe before nucleosynthesis?

My goal is to connect the microphysics and expansion history of the post-inflationary Universe with the nonlinear formation of structure, black-hole populations and gravitational-wave signals. This requires more than one matter model or one computational technique: analytic theory, numerical relativity and statistical inference constrain different parts of the same problem. Scalar fields provide controlled examples, but the programme also includes fluids, additional matter sectors, non-standard expansion histories and particle-physics motivated potentials.

RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

These directions share methods and physical questions, but are not dependent on all being pursued simultaneously. Each has a specific starting point in my previous or ongoing work.

01 / FOUNDATIONSCURRENT RESEARCH

Gravitational collapse beyond a single matter model

Which features of primordial-black-hole formation are universal, and which remember the matter sector or cosmological background?

I plan to investigate how primordial-black-hole formation changes across different early-Universe matter sectors and cosmological backgrounds, including scalar fields with different potentials, fluids with different equations of state, and coupled field–fluid systems. By studying collapse thresholds, critical exponents, self-similar behaviour and black-hole mass scaling, I aim to identify which features are universal and which retain information about the underlying physics. A central question is whether these differences can leave distinctive imprints on PBH mass distributions or other potentially observable signatures.

Physical objectiveIdentify universal and model-dependent features of PBH formation and assess their potential observational signatures.
Starting point: cosmological DSS ↗
02 / POST-INFLATIONARY UNIVERSEONGOING DIRECTION

Reheating, inflaton structures and early gravitational collapse

Can small-scale structure and black-hole formation distinguish prolonged reheating from a rapid transition to radiation domination?

During non-standard post-inflationary eras, perturbations can grow into bound inflaton structures, compact scalar cores or collapsing overdensities. I want to connect their formation, interactions, accretion and possible collapse to the underlying reheating dynamics, and determine the range in which these processes materially change PBH abundances or gravitational-wave predictions.

Physical objectiveConnect microscopic reheating assumptions to population-level and gravitational-wave constraints.
Starting point: PBHs during reheating ↗
03 / COSMOLOGICAL OBSERVABLESCURRENT RESEARCH

From critical collapse to PBH populations and gravitational waves

Which signatures survive the averaging from individual collapse events to realistic cosmological populations?

Nonlinear formation physics can alter mass functions even when the primordial spectrum is held fixed. A central direction is to propagate collapse maps—including DSS fine structure—through peak selection, profile distributions, primordial-spectrum widths and horizon-mass mixing, and relate the resulting PBH populations to induced stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds. An important challenge is separating information about the primordial fluctuations from information about the collapse process itself.

Physical objectiveIdentify robust, interpretable observables rather than features of a particular approximation.
Starting point: DSS mass functions ↗
04 / PARTICLE PHYSICS & SPACETIMEEXTENSION OF JOINT WORK

Metastable fields, vacuum dynamics and strong gravity

How does gravity determine the fate of regions that evolve beyond a metastable field barrier?

Our simulations of a Higgs-like spectator field reveal qualitatively different horizon geometries, including black-hole branches and transient wormhole-like behavior. I would like to test the dependence of these outcomes on the shape of the potential, initial conditions and cosmological environment, then assess which conclusions remain relevant for physically motivated particle-physics models. The connection to the actual Standard Model Higgs requires additional modelling, not simply a reinterpretation of the current toy potential.

Physical objectiveEstablish when vacuum instability is confined by black-hole formation and when spacetime branching may occur.
Starting point: Higgs-like collapse ↗
05 / COMPUTATIONAL METHODSDEVELOPING DIRECTION

Efficient numerical inference and physics-informed machine learning

How can a limited number of expensive relativistic simulations constrain a large space of initial conditions without losing physical reliability?

I am developing a direction in which numerical-relativity data are used to train and validate surrogate models for collapse versus dispersion, threshold location and black-hole mass. Active sampling near the threshold, held-out initial-data families and calibrated errors would be central. The goal is to accelerate population calculations while retaining direct numerical simulations as the reference, not to replace them with a black box.

Physical objectiveA reusable bridge between strong-gravity calculations and statistical cosmology.
Starting point: scalar-field simulations ↗
ADDITIONAL RESEARCH CONNECTION

Scalar-field dark matter and self-interacting structure

My earlier work on scalar-field dark matter, core–halo relations and compact structures provides another route to questions about nonlinear field dynamics. A complementary direction is to test how mass and self-interaction scale affect the properties and diversity of gravitationally bound scalar configurations, and how analytical constraints connect to astrophysical observables.

Academic background

I have worked in Mexico, the United Kingdom and Japan, collaborating on theoretical cosmology, numerical relativity and gravitational-wave physics.

My path

I trained in physics at the Universidad de Guadalajara and completed a PhD at CINVESTAV–IPN in Mexico in 2020, advised by Tonatiuh Matos. During my doctorate I spent six months at UT Austin working with Paul Shapiro and Tanja Rindler-Daller.

I then worked at UNAM with Juan Carlos Hidalgo, at Queen Mary University of London with David Mulryne and Karim Malik, and now at Rikkyo University in Tokyo with Tomohiro Harada. Each step broadened the tools and physical questions I could tackle.

I also teach and mentor students, develop numerical tools and collaborate on problems in cosmology, gravitation and high-energy theory.
